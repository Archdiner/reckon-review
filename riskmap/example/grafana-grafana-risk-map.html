How to read this

Five dimensions are shown rather than one risk score. A single number would invite an argument about weights and hide the reason a region surfaced; these can each be checked against the repository independently. Nothing here is summed or multiplied.

  • Orphaned — share of this region's commits made by contributors with no commit anywhere in the repository in the last 12 months. Activity is a repo-wide question: someone who moved to another area has not stopped being available to explain this one.
  • Concentration — share of commits from the single largest contributor. High concentration together with a high orphaned share is the bus-factor case.
  • Churn — commits and lines per month over the window. A region nobody touches is lower risk than a hot one with the same ownership profile.
  • Record coverage — share of mechanism questions about a change that the commit records actually answer, generated and scored by the same instrument used on the calibration corpus. p is the position in that corpus.
  • Last explanation — the most recent commit here whose message says anything beyond its own subject line.

Thresholds

A region is listed when it clears at least 3 of these tests. They are printed because a reader who disagrees with them should be able to see exactly what they were.

  • orphaned share ≥ 50%
  • concentration ≥ 50%
  • churn ≥ 3.8 commits / month
  • record coverage at or below the 30th percentile of the calibration corpus

Regions are directories at depth 3, chosen to give between 20 and 60 regions for this repository; directories with fewer than 15 commits are folded into their parent. Directory rather than file because renames make file-level history unreliable, and because four hundred cells is a screenshot rather than a decision.

Exclusions

  • Generated, vendored and lockfile paths are removed (9,081 file touches).
  • Bot authors — dependabot, renovate, release and CI accounts — are excluded entirely (3,569 commits). Left in, they dominate churn.
  • Commits touching more than 300 files are excluded from churn and authorship (27 commits). An initial import or vendor drop would otherwise make its author look like the owner of everything.
  • Migration, fixture, snapshot and locale directories are marked as low-meaning: high churn, little mechanism.
  • Contributor identities are merged across addresses before anything is counted (1563 author records → 1370 identities), because one person committing under three addresses would otherwise invent a bus factor that does not exist.

The departure proxy

No commits in 12 months is an inference, not a fact. People go on leave, change teams, move to another repository, or commit under an identity this tool failed to merge. That is why every figure is aggregated to a region, why the wording is inactivity rather than departure, and why no individual is named. Treat a flagged region as a question worth asking, not as a personnel finding.

Calibration

Record coverage is reported as a position against 1,000 merged pull requests from grafana/grafana, apache/airflow, supabase/supabase, langchain-ai/langchain and prisma/prisma, scored with the same question generator and rubric.

55.7% of that corpus scores exactly zero, so the bottom five deciles are all zero and a "bottom decile" claim would be fiction. Percentiles here are midranks, and a value inside the zero mass is reported as tied rather than ranked.

Regions of different sizes are compared without normalising, because coverage was measured to be flat across a tenfold range of change size — under 3 points of movement, against 25-26 points for a measure that does respond to size.

One mismatch worth knowing: the corpus scores pull-request records (description plus commit messages); this tool scores commit messages only, because a clone is its whole dependency and descriptions are not in it. A commit-only record is a subset, so these percentiles are biased low. A region that looks fine is fine; a region that looks bad may partly be the missing channel.
